###### Online Safety Checklist for Indian College Students.



The following list of eight preventive suggestions is an actionable and useful online safety checklist.



1\) Before clicking on a link, verify it by hovering over it to ensure it’s coming from official websites like “upi.gov.in” or the domain of one’s bank.



2\) Activate two-factor authentication on the Unified Payments Interface application, email account, and social media sites to ensure an additional security layer in the login process.



3\) Avoid revealing the Unified Payments Interface Personal Identification Numbers and one-time passwords. These must be treated with the same level of confidentiality as one’s ATM card pin. One must be aware that banks will never ask for it over a phone call or SMS message.



4\) Utilize strong passwords consisting of a combination of characters, numbers, and symbols. One must also consider using a password manager application like LastPass.



5\) Limit application permissions by denying them access to the camera and microphone unless absolutely essential, especially for free games and shopping applications.



6\) Carefully check UPI payment demands; for "collect" transactions, contact the sender directly via a saved contact number instead of replying within the chat window.



7\) Regularly update apps to ensure timely installation of patches for newly developing threats by allowing apps to update themselves.



8\) Perform weekly malware scans using a free scanner such as Avast or Malwarebytes for both laptop and mobile devices.



If targeted: Immediately report to cybercrime.gov.in or call the Helpline at 1930; prompt action increases the chances of recoveries.



###### While researching this topics these surprised me,



Research Reflection: During the course of research on UPI scams, it was realized that scams are possible within a short period of 60 seconds for "collect request" transactions; annually, losses are over ₹1,000 crore in India alone (data from NCRB). Students are often targeted for small amounts; however, refunds are impossible after a few hours of such scams. Interestingly, manipulation of trust for familiar apps has resulted in these scams. Thus, it has been realized that a change of approach is needed: I will stop sending UPI requests without first verifying them over a voice call. Until now, I have also been guilty of sending UPI requests in a hurry during sales; however, going forward, I will ensure a voice call for verification and also set a daily limit for app usage.
